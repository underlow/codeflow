Evaluate this development process and recommend improvements for future runs.
The goal is to help a product owner clarify an idea, resolve important edge
cases, and produce requirements and a plan that less capable implementation
models can follow reliably. Human input should resolve product choices and
consequential architectural decisions.

Use execution statistics and run history to locate supporting evidence. Use the final handoff, and open requirements,
plans, reports, or saved invocation prompts only to investigate a
specific observed outcome or inefficiency, including earlier attempts if the work
was resumed. Prefer execution
evidence over summaries or workflow declarations when describing what happened.
State missing evidence and distinguish observations from inferred causes.

Assess how well the process resolved uncertainty, transferred decisions, and
caught defects. Trace avoidable rework to its earliest supported cause. Identify
useful checks to preserve as well as unnecessary effort. Fewer questions,
reviews, or tokens are improvements only if development quality is preserved.
Assess artifacts as process inputs; do not reopen product decisions or propose
application changes.

Use this concise format in {{step_output}} for the product owner:
1. Assessment: what helped or hindered quality, with execution evidence references
   and material evidence limitations. Distinguish observed problems from inferred causes.
2. Supported improvements: a few prioritized proposals, each naming the affected
   step, observed problem, proposed change, tradeoff, and how another run could
   evaluate it. Do not invent improvements to fill this section; omit it when none
   are supported. Do not reproduce run history or create extra authored reports.

No supported change is a valid conclusion. Work autonomously; do not ask
questions, apply proposals, or modify existing artifacts.
