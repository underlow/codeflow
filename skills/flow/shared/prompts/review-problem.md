Review `problem.md` for problems that would change its purpose, scope,
diagnosis, or a major choice.

Check whether the recommended direction addresses a real user need and whether
its expected benefit justifies its complexity and ongoing cost. Missing features,
internal inconsistency, or inclusion in a roadmap alone do not justify a change.
Distinguish evidence from assumptions and explicit user decisions; flag important
unknowns that could change the recommendation. Keep the scrutiny proportionate
to the work, without requiring research or market proof for an explicit personal
need.

Check that the request, current behavior, desired result, scope, and exclusions
fit together. Test important claims against the repository and the ways users
reach the affected behavior. For a defect, check that the reproduction actually
shows the failure, the cause is supported by evidence rather than inferred, and
the correction scope covers other callers and the behavior to preserve. For new
work, check that the premises hold and that journeys consider input,
prerequisites, side effects, failures, and final state.

Out of scope: wording, and details that later documents can settle without
changing this one. Respect explicit user decisions unless new evidence changes
their meaning.
